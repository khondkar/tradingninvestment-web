The Nasdaq Fell 13.48%. How Does Today’s AI Sell-Off Compare With the Dot-Com Crash?

The Nasdaq has entered correction territory, and investors are questioning whether AI infrastructure spending can earn adequate returns. The sell-off raises a bubble question; it does not answer it.

By Kamal Khondkar · Quantitative Research

The Nasdaq Composite closed at 23,879.20 on November 19, down 13.48% from its October 6 record close of 27,599.79. The index fell 2.25% on November 19 alone. A decline above 10% is commonly called a correction; the label describes the size of the fall, not its cause or what happens next.

Why the AI sell-off has revived the bubble question

Investors are reassessing whether the revenue and productivity gains from artificial intelligence will justify the cost of building data centers, buying accelerators and expanding power capacity. Recent reporting has identified high valuations and heavy spending as concerns weighing on AI shares.

The pressure is occurring alongside strong company results. NVIDIA reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110%. Those figures show that demand is producing substantial revenue for a leading supplier; they do not yet settle the return earned by every customer investing in AI.

LPL Research estimated that four major hyperscalers were guiding toward roughly $730 billion of capital spending in 2026, about 78% above the roughly $410 billion spent in 2025. That is broad company capital expenditure, not an audited tally of AI-only investment.

The test is whether customer revenue, productivity and free cash flow rise fast enough to support that buildout. If investment keeps growing while monetization, utilization or returns disappoint, investors may lower the multiples they are willing to pay. If revenue and cash flow keep pace, heavy spending may be more sustainable than the sell-off suggests.

What would confirm a deeper AI bear market?

One sharp decline cannot establish that today’s market is a bubble. More useful evidence will arrive in company results: whether cloud and AI revenue growth keeps pace with capital spending, whether customers report measurable returns, and whether free cash flow remains resilient as infrastructure costs rise.

Investors can also watch earnings revisions, backlog conversion, data-center utilization, financing needs and market breadth. A broadening decline accompanied by weaker estimates would suggest more than a valuation reset in a few crowded trades. Improving cash generation and sustained demand would challenge the bearish case.

The technology can be right while the price is wrong

The dot-com era remains relevant because a technology can transform the economy while many stocks tied to it still deliver poor returns from an inflated entry price. The comparison is a risk framework, not a forecast that the Nasdaq must repeat its 2000–02 path.

As of November 19, the Nasdaq was 13.48% below its record close. Nvidia’s sales show that AI demand is real, while the scale of planned spending makes the return-on-investment question harder to ignore. The bubble thesis will depend on whether revenue and cash flows catch up with expectations—not on the sell-off alone.
